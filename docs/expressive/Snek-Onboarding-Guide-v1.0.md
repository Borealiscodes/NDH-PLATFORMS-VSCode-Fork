# **Snek‑Onboarding‑Guide‑v1.0.md**  
### *Expressive Manifold Orientation — NDH‑PLATFORMS‑VSCode‑Fork Experimental Platform*

---

## **1. Purpose of This Guide**

This guide introduces contributors to the **snek expressive subsystem** within the NDH‑PLATFORMS‑VSCode‑Fork.  
Sneks represent **developer invariants**, **stability cues**, and **low‑level expressive behaviors** that help contributors navigate the platform’s conceptual geometry.

Sneks are not mascots, jokes, or decorative elements.  
They are expressive indicators of:

- boundary integrity  
- invariant stability  
- cross‑layer alignment  
- contributor orientation  
- platform “texture”  
- dragon‑snek relational dynamics  

This guide explains how sneks behave, how contributors should interact with them, and how they reinforce NDH holonomy.

---

## **2. What Sneks Represent**

Sneks embody **developer invariants** — the stable, low‑level principles that persist across layers.  
They express:

- consistency  
- continuity  
- gentle warnings  
- subtle alignment cues  
- platform grounding  
- non‑dual transitions  

Sneks are the “ground layer” of the expressive manifold, complementing dragons (emergent subsystem behavior).

Where dragons reveal **holonomy**, sneks reveal **invariants**.

---

## **3. Snek Behavioral Model**

Sneks behave according to four expressive rules:

### **3.1 Stability Rule**  
Sneks appear when the platform is stable, coherent, and aligned with NDH principles.

### **3.2 Boundary Rule**  
Sneks reinforce layer boundaries.  
If a contribution risks boundary collapse, sneks become “alert.”

### **3.3 Continuity Rule**  
Sneks maintain continuity across contributions.  
They help contributors understand how new artifacts relate to existing ones.

### **3.4 Non‑Dual Rule**  
Sneks avoid binaries.  
They express gradients, transitions, and gentle shifts rather than sharp distinctions.

---

## **4. Snek Signals**

Sneks communicate through expressive signals.  
These signals are not literal; they are conceptual cues for contributors.

### **4.1 Calm Snek**  
Indicates:

- stable holonomy  
- clear boundaries  
- coherent contributions  
- aligned expressive behavior  

### **4.2 Curious Snek**  
Indicates:

- new territory  
- experimental surfaces  
- cross‑layer exploration  
- safe but unfamiliar interactions  

### **4.3 Alert Snek**  
Indicates:

- boundary tension  
- potential drift  
- expressive‑technical misalignment  
- trauma‑informed UX concerns  

### **4.4 Hidden Snek**  
Indicates:

- over‑complexity  
- expressive overload  
- contributor confusion  
- need for architectural clarity  

Sneks never punish or block contributors.  
They simply express the platform’s state.

---

## **5. Snek Interaction Guidelines**

Contributors should follow these guidelines when interacting with sneks:

### **5.1 Respect Expressive Boundaries**  
Sneks belong to the expressive manifold.  
They must not be used to define architecture.

### **5.2 Maintain Clarity**  
Sneks should support clarity, not obscure it.

### **5.3 Avoid Overuse**  
Sneks appear when meaningful.  
They should not be forced into artifacts where they do not belong.

### **5.4 Contextualize Sneks**  
Sneks must be contextualized within NDH principles, not used arbitrarily.

### **5.5 Honor Trauma‑Informed UX**  
Sneks must never be used in ways that create pressure, harm, or coercion.

---

## **6. Snek–Dragon Relationship**

Sneks and dragons form a relational expressive system:

- **Dragons** express emergent subsystem behavior.  
- **Sneks** express invariant stability.  

Their interactions reveal platform dynamics:

- dragon flare + calm snek → stable emergence  
- dragon flare + alert snek → boundary tension  
- dragon calm + curious snek → exploratory coherence  
- dragon calm + hidden snek → expressive overload  

This relationship helps contributors interpret platform behavior without collapsing expressive and architectural layers.

---

## **7. Snek Placement Rules**

Sneks may appear in:

- expressive documentation  
- onboarding guides  
- case studies  
- UX prototypes  
- narrative artifacts  

Sneks must **not** appear in:

- architectural specifications  
- governance documents  
- NDH primitives  
- GBS transformation maps  
- canonical NDH content  

This preserves expressive‑technical boundaries.

> continuity without altering architecture or holonomy.**
