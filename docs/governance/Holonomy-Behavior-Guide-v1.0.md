# **Holonomy‑Behavior‑Guide‑v1.0.md**  
### *Governance Note — NDH‑PLATFORMS‑VSCode‑Fork Experimental Platform*

---

## **1. Purpose of This Governance Note**

This document defines the **holonomy governance model** for the NDH‑PLATFORMS‑VSCode‑Fork.  
Holonomy is the platform’s measure of **coherence**, **boundary integrity**, and **cross‑layer stability**.  
It ensures that NDH experimentation remains aligned with the platform’s architecture and does not collapse into domain absorption or drift.

This guide applies to all layers referenced in the **Platform Blueprint Suite** and enforced through **CONTRIBUTING.md**.

---

## **2. Definition of Holonomy**

Holonomy, within NDH architecture, is the **behavior of the platform as a whole** when any part of it is changed.  
It measures:

- coherence across layers  
- stability of invariants  
- integrity of boundaries  
- expressive‑technical alignment  
- trauma‑informed UX compliance  
- GBS transformation impact  

Holonomy is not a rule — it is a **governance field** that ensures the platform behaves consistently as it evolves.

---

## **3. Holonomy Principles**

### **3.1 Coherence Over Correctness**  
Holonomy prioritizes **coherence** rather than correctness.  
A contribution may be technically correct but still destabilize the platform if it violates coherence.

### **3.2 Boundary Integrity**  
Each layer — NDH primitives, GBS experiments, UX prototypes, expressive manifold — must remain distinct.  
Cross‑layer contamination is considered holonomy drift.

### **3.3 Non‑Dual Workflows**  
Holonomy rejects binary logic.  
Contributors must use gradients, spectra, and contextual reasoning.

### **3.4 Trauma‑Informed Constraints**  
Holonomy requires dignity‑aligned UX and interaction patterns.  
Any contribution that introduces harm, pressure, or coercion is holonomy‑negative.

### **3.5 Expressive‑Technical Harmony**  
Dragons, sneks, and expressive artifacts must support — not override — architectural clarity.

### **3.6 Canonical Protection**  
Holonomy forbids accidental migration of experimental artifacts into NDH‑PLATFORMS canonical.

---

## **4. Holonomy Metrics**

Holonomy is evaluated using six metrics:

### **4.1 Coherence Metric**  
Does the contribution strengthen or weaken the platform’s conceptual alignment?

### **4.2 Boundary Metric**  
Does the contribution respect layer boundaries?

### **4.3 Drift Metric**  
Does the contribution introduce unintended conceptual drift?

### **4.4 Interference Metric**  
Does the contribution cause cross‑layer interference?

### **4.5 Dignity Metric**  
Does the contribution maintain trauma‑informed UX principles?

### **4.6 Expressive Metric**  
Does expressive content support clarity rather than overwhelm it?

These metrics are qualitative, not numerical.

---

## **5. Holonomy Checkpoints for Contributors**

Every contribution must include a holonomy note addressing:

- **Layer identification**  
- **Expected coherence impact**  
- **Boundary considerations**  
- **Potential drift vectors**  
- **Cross‑layer interactions**  
- **Trauma‑informed UX implications**  
- **Expressive manifold relevance**  

These checkpoints are required for all contributions, including:

- NDH primitives  
- GBS experiments  
- UX prototypes  
- extensions  
- expressive artifacts  
- case studies  

---

## **6. Holonomy Failure Modes**

Holonomy failures occur when contributions violate coherence or boundaries.  
Common failure modes include:

### **6.1 Domain Absorption**  
One layer attempts to consume another (e.g., expressive artifacts becoming architectural specs).

### **6.2 Layer Collapse**  
Two layers merge unintentionally (e.g., GBS logic embedded directly into NDH primitives).

### **6.3 Expressive Overload**  
Dragons, sneks, or metaphors overshadow architectural clarity.

### **6.4 Canonical Contamination**  
Experimental artifacts leak into NDH‑PLATFORMS canonical.

### **6.5 UX Harm**  
Trauma‑informed constraints are violated.

### **6.6 Drift Cascade**  
A small misalignment triggers larger coherence failures across layers.

---

## **7. Holonomy Recovery Procedures**

When holonomy drift is detected, contributors must:

### **7.1 Identify the Drift Vector**  
Which layer caused the misalignment?

### **7.2 Restore Boundary Integrity**  
Move or refactor the artifact into the correct layer.

### **7.3 Re‑evaluate Coherence**  
Ensure the fix does not introduce new drift.

### **7.4 Document the Recovery**  
Add a holonomy recovery note to the commit.

### **7.5 Update Case Studies if Needed**  
If the drift produced meaningful insights, document them in:

- **White Paper case studies**

---

## **8. Dragon Holonomy Model**

Dragons represent **emergent subsystem behavior**.  
Their role in holonomy governance is to:

- reveal coherence  
- signal drift  
- highlight boundary violations  
- express platform “mood”  
- warn of interference  
- stabilize expressive‑technical alignment  

A “dragon flare” is any moment where emergent behavior reveals a holonomy issue.

A “dragon calm” indicates stable coherence.

Dragons do not enforce rules — they **express** the platform’s state.

Sneks represent developer invariants and respond to dragon behavior.

---

## **9. Holonomy Governance Workflow**

### **Step 1 — Prototype**  
Contributor creates an artifact in the correct layer.

### **Step 2 — Holonomy Note**  
Contributor documents expected coherence impact.

### **Step 3 — Review**  
Holonomy reviewer checks for drift, boundary issues, and UX constraints.

### **Step 4 — Integration**  
Artifact is merged into the experimental platform.

### **Step 5 — Observation**  
Dragon behavior is monitored for emergent signals.

### **Step 6 — Case Study (optional)**  
If emergence occurs, document it in the White Paper staging surface.
